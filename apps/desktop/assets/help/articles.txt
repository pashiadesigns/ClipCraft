keywords: start, overview, introduction, tour, basics

ClipDesigner captures your screen as images or videos, lets you mark them up, and turns them into guides, GIFs and videos. Everything you add stays editable until you export.

step: Export from the Export tab, or copy with Ctrl+C. | title: Export or copy | icon: Save As | pic: welcome-4

tip: Press F1 while a tooltip is showing to open the article about that control.

id: import-image
section: Images

keywords: svg, eps, vector, logo, import, insert picture, file, ghostscript
hero: import-image-hero
---
Image from file adds a picture as an image object you can move, resize, crop and tilt.

step: On the Insert tab, open the menu under Paste. | title: Open the Paste menu | pic: import-image-1
step: Choose Image from file… and pick your file. | title: Pick your file

An SVG comes in as a vector icon: its shapes stay sharp at any size, and each of its colors (up to five) is its own swatch in Properties, just like ClipDesigner's built-in icons. Importing an SVG into Icons works the same way. An SVG with pictures inside or more than five colors stays a drawing instead; it is still sharp, and Recolor draws it in one color. art: svg | The same SVG at two sizes. Both are sharp.

EPS files are PostScript. If Ghostscript is installed, ClipDesigner uses it to draw the EPS in high resolution. Without it, ClipDesigner uses the small preview stored in the file and tells you.

When you export to PDF, SVG drawings stay shapes. Only the parts under a redaction, blur or pixelation become pictures, with the covered area removed from the file.

# New from PDF
id: new-from-pdf
section: Images
where: File › New › From PDF
controls: From PDF, New from PDF, Change file…, Editable pages, Pictures, Scan after import, Include all parts, Previous part, Next part, PDF pages…, Insert PDF pages, Searchable text, This part, All parts, Skip to next part
keywords: pdf, document, pages, import, open pdf, redact pdf, guide, acrobat
---
New from PDF turns the pages of a PDF into a guide, one PDF page per guide page, sized to the PDF. Every image tool works on them as usual: Steps, Focus, Magnify, Obfuscate, Blocks and the rest.

step: Open File › New and choose From PDF, or drop a PDF on the window. | title: Choose a PDF
step: Click pages to include or leave them out, or type a range such as 1-3, 6. The arrow keys step through the preview. | title: Pick pages
step: Choose Editable pages or Pictures, then Create guide. | title: Create the guide

Editable pages stay sharp at any zoom and keep the PDF's own words, so Find Sensitive Info matches the real text exactly. A page that can't be rebuilt faithfully, such as a scan, comes in as a picture, and ClipDesigner tells you once which pages did. Pictures turns every page into one image at the resolution you choose; its text is read with OCR.

Turn on Scan after import to look for sensitive information as soon as the guide is ready. Findings open in the usual review, and nothing is covered until you choose.

A password-protected PDF asks for its password. ClipDesigner doesn't keep the password, and the guide is not protected. A damaged file, or one that isn't a PDF, imports nothing.

One guide holds about 24 Letter pages. A longer PDF comes in as linked parts, such as "Vendor packet · part 1 of 10", grouped in the Library; Part 1 of 10 in the footer moves between them. When you export a part, Include all parts in one file puts every part back together: one PDF, PowerPoint or Word file, or numbered pictures for PNG and JPEG. Find Sensitive Info can scan This part or All parts; with All parts, each part's findings are reviewed in turn, and applying or skipping them opens the next part.

When you export to PDF, editable pages stay shapes. Where a redaction, blur or pixelation touches a page, only what is under it becomes a picture, and the covered words are removed from the file, not hidden. The project keeps the original pages, so share exports, not projects.

To add pages to a guide you already have, open the Insert tab's Paste menu and choose PDF pages…. Pick the pages, then choose where they go and whether they keep their PDF size or take the size of the guide's pages. It's one Undo step.

PDF export keeps the words of PDF pages searchable, so they can be found and copied in a PDF viewer. Words under a redaction, blur or pixelation are always removed. Turn off Searchable text in Export to leave it out.

ClipDesigner doesn't edit the PDF's own text, fill forms, sign, or keep bookmarks, links and PDF comments. Edit text in a PDF editor first, and sign last, after exporting from ClipDesigner.

# PowerPoint and Word

PowerPoint makes one slide for each guide page, or for each step section. # Share with your team
id: sharing
section: Export
where: File › Export › Share a kit, File › Open › Add a kit, Preferences › Team
controls: Share a kit…, Add a kit…, Kit name, Save kit…, Same names, Keep both, Replace mine, Team, Browse…, Clear, Lock projects I open from shared folders, Show my name as, Open a copy, Pack project
keywords: kit, cdkit, team, share presets, styles, collaborate, lock, shared drive, google drive, onedrive, sharepoint, pack, cdpack, send project
---
A kit is one .cdkit file with your presets, Blocks, color palettes, custom sensitive types, and if you like your watermarks and templates. Send it any way you like. Whoever opens it sees what it adds and chooses; nothing changes until they click Add. When a name is already used, Keep both adds a numbered copy and Replace mine replaces it.

Preferences › Team can point at a shared folder of kits, such as a Google Drive, network, Teams or SharePoint folder. Kits there are added automatically, and again whenever someone updates one. ClipDesigner never changes the folder; to change a team kit, share a new one.

A project opened from a shared folder gets a small lock file, so others see who has it and since when, and it opens read-only for them. Open a copy makes your own copy in the Library. The lock clears when you close ClipDesigner, or after 12 hours if a PC went offline.

To hand a project to someone, choose Pack project in Save As: the project and all its media go in one .cdpack file. Opening it unpacks a normal project into a new folder beside it.
